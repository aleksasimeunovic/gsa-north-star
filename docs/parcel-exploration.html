GSA North Star · parcel delineation

Changing the river field, from the flag to the confirmed parcel

Three ways Milan could split his field, why one was chosen, and how the decision reaches the rest of the application. Prepared for Earl's review of 31 October.

The scenario

The satellite photo shows the lower strip of the river field greening later than the wheat above it. Milan knows why: that part floods every spring, so this year it is sunflowers. Today he would have to redraw the field with Jelena (P6). He knows exactly where the crop changes, but he has never needed a cadastral number and is unsure of the old family computer.

The task has to work on a phone at 360 px wide, for someone in his sixties, with a finger. The full interaction is built into the prototype. Road strip is the single exception, and adding land stays a small supporting flow.

Three ways to split the field

Rough sketches of step 4, the hard part. All three start from the same flagged field on a phone.

Option A · the previous build

Slide one straight line

  • Very little to learn, and one finger is enough.
  • Water doesn't run in straight lines, so the area is wrong wherever the edge curves.
  • He has no way to say what he knows about the shape.
Option B · the GIS way

Tap points along the boundary

  • Exact, and familiar to anyone who knows GIS.
  • Starts from nothing, so GSA's evidence goes unused.
  • Needs precise placement with a finger, the thing Earl asked to find alternatives to.
  • Opens every invalid shape: stray points, crossings, a line that never closes.
Option C · chosen and built

Pull GSA's line into place

  • Starts from GSA's work, so the usual effort is one tap on Continue.
  • Follows a curved boundary with only a few bends.
  • He presses a line, a big target, instead of placing a small point.
  • Costs more to explain than A, and more to build.

These are design judgements, not results. Nobody has tried any of the three yet.

Earl's questions, answered in the prototype

QuestionWhat happens
How does Milan select the area?He doesn't have to. GSA has already marked it from the photo, and he confirms it by answering the question. In step 5 he taps a part on the map to choose its crop.
How does he draw or adjust the line?He presses anywhere on the line and pulls, and it bends there. Round handles move the bends. Square handles at the ends slide along the field's edge and can't leave it. A slider moves the whole line, and arrow keys move any handle.
Can the interface help follow a boundary?The line starts on the edge in the photo. A bend within 6 m of that edge joins it. "Follow the edge in the photo" returns to it, and "Make the line straight" suits a ploughed line.
What if he places a point incorrectly?He never places one, so there is nothing to misplace. A bend he doesn't want can be dragged away, taken out, removed with Delete, or undone. A bend let go on a straight stretch disappears by itself.
How does he undo?Undo walks back one move at a time during the task. After confirming, the application's Undo reverses the whole split, and "Change the split" reopens it.
How does GSA prevent invalid geometry?A move that would leave a bad line stops at the last good position, and the panel says why at that moment: the field's edge, the line crossing itself, or a part under 0.10 ha. An invalid shape can't exist, so no error appears later.

The exception: Milan disagrees about the road

The land map marks a 0.12 ha strip of the road field as road. Milan inspects the strip on the photo with its size and payment impact, and answers. If he says it is still his field, GSA keeps both areas, 1.68 ha on the land map and 1.80 ha from him, and answers what he will want to know.

Assumptions to confirm with the ministry

Each is labelled in the prototype. Open Prototype and tick "Show design notes" to see them on screen.

  • A part of a parcel must be at least 0.10 ha. This number is a placeholder.
  • Land with nothing sown doesn't count for basic support for plant production.
  • A field can be split into two parts with different crops at the same rate per hectare.
  • An application can be submitted while a boundary correction is being checked, and the land map's area counts until then.
  • A correction is settled by the land registry, and the result can reach GSA.
What this does not show yet

None of this has been tested with farmers, so it supports no claim about improved usability. The edge GSA proposes is drawn into the scenario by hand, so it is a stand-in for real detection and doesn't line up with what is visible on the actual photo. Fields are simple four-sided shapes, and a real parcel with a concave outline would need more geometry.
